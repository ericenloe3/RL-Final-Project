What the entropy fixes actually did
PPO — clamp confirmed working, but only the hunter improved
The training panel shows the PPO log_std clamp behaved exactly as designed. Looking at the per-update logs:

Hunter log_std raw stayed in [-1.4, +0.0] throughout training, well below the +0.5 upper bound. The clamp never engaged for the hunter — proving it doesn't interfere with normal convergence.
Prey log_std raw climbed from +0.10 at step 40k to +0.501 by step 860k and pinned there for the remaining 9.1M steps. Without the clamp it would have continued climbing past 26 (the v6 failure value). With the clamp, prey entropy froze at 3.838 nats — the maximum possible value at log_std=0.5.

So the clamp is doing its job in the literal sense: the prey is no longer sampling Gaussian noise on a 10¹¹ scale. It's sampling on a scale of ~1.65, which is large but bounded. Yet the prey's actual return is essentially identical to v5's (~−87 vs −89). Why?
The prey hit the upper bound and stayed there, which means the entropy gradient from the loss is always pushing log_std upward at the boundary, even when the policy gradient says otherwise. The clamp prevents the runaway, but it doesn't restore healthy training — the prey's policy is still effectively dominated by its noise term, just at a smaller fixed magnitude. The mean network's gradients have to compete with a constant high-amplitude exploration signal that never decays.
What this tells us: the prey's ENT_COEF["prey"]["end"] = 0.005 is still too high relative to the magnitude of the prey's return signal. The prey is in a losing equilibrium (caught nearly every episode) so its advantages are mostly negative and small. The entropy bonus dominates, log_std saturates the cap, and the policy can't sharpen. The hunter, by contrast, is clearly winning (advantages are large and positive), so its policy gradient overcomes the entropy bonus and log_std descends naturally to −1.4.
The fix worked as a safety mechanism — no more exploding numerics — but it didn't address the deeper imbalance. To get the prey to actually train, the entropy coefficient needs to come down further (to maybe 0.001 or zero by end of training), or the prey needs to win sometimes so its policy gradient has positive advantages to lock onto.
SAC — α floor catches the collapse, but the policy still locks in
The SAC training panel is the more interesting story. The α curve is qualitatively different from before:

α started at 0.2 instead of 1.0 (visible immediately on the chart).
Both α values descended toward the floor of 0.02 as designed.
Hunter α didn't crash to zero — it stayed in [0.05, 0.11] for the first 4M steps, only reaching the floor at step 5.19M.
Both α values are now exactly pinned at 0.02 at the end (vs ~0.005 for prey and ~0.005 for hunter in v5).

The floor mechanism works perfectly. The system never goes into the truly degenerate state where α essentially disappears.
But look at what happened with capture rate. SAC peaked at 100% at step 1.18M (vs 1.45M in v5), then declined more slowly — crossed 50% at step 3.66M, kept descending, stabilised around 4–8% in the middle, and rose to 13.4% by the end. The qualitative collapse pattern is identical to v5, just shifted in time. Same shape, same destination, slightly different timing.
The live entropy data shows what's happening: hunter live entropy fell to near-zero (−0.14 at step 4.6M, the bottom) while α was still at 0.034. Even with α held at 0.02, the entropy bonus is 0.02 × log_prob ≈ 0.02 × (−1) = −0.02, which is a vanishingly small term in an actor loss where Q-values are in the 10s to 100s. The floor of 0.02 is too low to actually drive exploration once the policy has converged to a deterministic mean.
The SAC prey, however, did benefit. Its return climbed from −60 at step 1M back up to +27 by step 8M, and its live entropy actually grew over the second half of training (from 0.1 to 0.5 nats). The asymmetric outcome — winning prey, losing hunter — is the opposite of PPO's asymmetric outcome — winning hunter, losing prey. In both methods, the agent that's currently winning gets a clean policy gradient and trains effectively, while the losing agent's policy gradient is too weak to overcome the noise/entropy floor.

The deeper pattern across both runs
There's a structural insight worth highlighting. In zero-sum co-evolution, whichever agent has a clearer reward signal at any given moment trains effectively, and the other agent's training is dominated by its exploration mechanism. The exploration mechanism (PPO entropy bonus, SAC α-temperature) is supposed to be a small steady push, but in the losing agent's case it becomes the dominant term in the loss because the policy gradient is too weak to overcome it.
This explains why both fixes "worked" in the narrow technical sense (no numerical explosion, no degenerate α=0) but neither shifted the final equilibrium. The fixes addressed symptoms — the wrong form of failure — without changing the underlying co-evolutionary dynamics that determine which agent wins.
The eval comparison panel confirms this with deterministic policies: PPO captures 100% of episodes (15/15), SAC captures 13% (2/15). PPO's hunter closing distance is a clean 410 px on average (closing the full distance), SAC's hunter actively recedes in some episodes (negative distance closed). The SAC prey breaks LOS substantially more (mean 14.5 vs PPO's 1.0) which corroborates that the SAC prey is the one that successfully trained — it's now genuinely good at evasion against a hunter that can't pursue.

What the diagnostics tell you about next steps
The diagnostic logging from this run is exactly what you want to have when planning the next iteration. Two specific signals stand out:
For PPO, the prey log_std being saturated at +0.500 for 9.1M of 10M steps is a clear signal that ENT_COEF["prey"]["end"] is still too high. A reasonable next move would be to anneal it to 0.001 or even 0, and possibly drop the upper clamp to +0.0 (limiting std to exp(0)=1.0) to give the policy gradient more relative weight at the boundary.
For SAC, the hunter α hitting the 0.02 floor at step 5.19M and capture rate falling from there is the key event. Two options: lower the floor to 0.005 to let the temperature shrink further (which might let the hunter sharpen its policy), or — and this is more interesting — the actual problem might not be α at all. The hunter's live entropy being −0.14 means the policy IS deterministic; the problem is just that the deterministic policy is bad. Co-adaptation has driven the hunter into a poor local optimum that more exploration won't escape from — what would help is something like buffer diversification (occasional resets, or sampling from older policies' replay data) rather than more entropy.
