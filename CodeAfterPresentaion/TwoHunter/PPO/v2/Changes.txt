Summary of what was changed and why
Diagnosis (what your data showed)
The training log told a clean story: capture rate hit 100% by step ~3.5M and stayed there, with prey return locked at −90 (the capture penalty floor) for the entire second half of training. The prey's log_std saturated the +0.5 upper clamp at step 200k and stayed pinned there for 9.8M of 10M total steps. The clamp was preventing numerical explosion as designed, but the prey was effectively training on pure noise: every advantage was negative (every episode a loss), which gave the policy gradient zero useful directional information, and the entropy bonus dominated the loss to push log_std up against the cap. Then it stayed there.
The eval GIFs corroborate this with deterministic policies: 15/15 captures with avg 147 steps. Looking at the GIFs, the prey runs in mostly straight lines and gets cornered against walls and obstacles — none of the cover-seeking behavior that emerged in 1v1.
On your episode-length question
Shortening max_steps does not make the contest fairer — the per-step game dynamics are identical regardless of episode length, and a captured prey gets the same reward whether episode 1 ends at step 130 or step 600. What it does affect is training-signal density. With shorter episodes, more episodes complete per PPO update (each update sees more terminal rewards), so the prey's gradient signal updates faster when winning episodes do occur. I lowered max_steps 600 → 450, which is the right move for that reason but not for the fairness reason you suggested.
Five fixes applied
1. Speed curriculum re-introduced (env.py + train.py). This is the single biggest change. The previous version had hunters and prey at 4.5/4.5 throughout — a brutally hard starting point that guaranteed the prey lost every episode from random initialization. v2 starts hunters at 4.0 (slower than prey's 4.5) for the first 15% of training, ramps to 4.2 → 4.4 → 4.5 over the remaining phases. The early prey-favored window is essential: it gives the prey positive advantages from successful escapes that the policy gradient can lock onto, building cover-seeking behavior before the contest reaches equal speed at step 7M.
2. Per-role log_std clamp bounds (train.py). The class now takes log_std_min and log_std_max as constructor arguments. Hunter keeps [-3, +0.5] (it never saturated). Prey gets [-3, 0.0], capping action std at exp(0) = 1.0 instead of 1.65. This halves the noise headroom and makes it easier for the policy gradient to dominate the entropy bonus.
3. Prey end-of-training entropy coefficient lowered 0.005 → 0.001. Combined with the lower clamp, this reduces the upward pressure on log_std during late training, so the prey can actually anneal to a sharp committed policy instead of remaining at maximum noise.
4. max_steps lowered 600 → 450 (env.py). Training-signal density argument above. 450 is still ~2× the typical successful 1v1 episode (~225 steps) so it remains generous, but completes ~33% more episodes per update.
5. Diagnostic logging unchanged. The *SAT* saturation marker now uses the per-role upper bound, so the prey's marker triggers at +0.0 and the hunter's at +0.5. If the prey saturates again you'll see it immediately.
What you should see in the next run
With the speed curriculum, capture rate should be near zero for the first 1-2M steps — that's the prey-favored phase doing its job, giving the prey winning episodes. Capture rate then climbs as hunter speed increases, ideally settling at something non-trivial but well below 100% (something like 60-80% would indicate a genuine equilibrium where both agents have policies worth playing). The prey's log_std should descend from +0.0 toward something more committed (around -1.0 to -0.5), mirroring the healthy curve the hunter showed in the previous run. Per-hunter capture splits should stay close to 50/50 if parameter sharing continues to work.
If the prey still saturates the new lower clamp throughout training, the next move would be to drop the prey's network capacity slightly (192 → 128) to reduce its representational power and force a tighter policy.
