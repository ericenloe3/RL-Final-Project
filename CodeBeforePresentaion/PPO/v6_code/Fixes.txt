Only env.py changed. Here's what and why:
The root cause was the displacement-scaled heading formula from v4. At a wall, the prey's actual displacement drops to zero (clamped by the boundary). The v4 formula multiplied heading reward by min(1, displacement / rated_speed), which zeroed out the entire directional signal at walls. The prey literally received identical reward (zero) regardless of whether it tried to slide along the wall toward open space, push into the wall, or turn around. With no gradient, the policy defaulted to whatever it did most during training — which was pushing into the corner.
Fix 1 — Revert to the original heading formula: dot(displacement, escape_direction) / rated_speed. This naturally gives reduced-but-nonzero reward at walls. A prey sliding along the bottom wall toward open space gets +0.0005/step heading reward. That's tiny compared to the +0.11/step in open space, but it's a real gradient pointing toward "slide this way." The v4 formula gave exactly 0.0 for every direction at the wall — no gradient at all.
Fix 2 — Strengthen wall penalty with corner detection: The v4 wall penalty (-0.02 within 15px) was 5× too weak to matter against even the reduced heading reward. Now it's -0.05 within 30px of any edge, plus an additional -0.05 when near two edges simultaneously (corner). The net reward breakdown:

This creates a clear three-tier gradient: open space is strongly positive, edges are mildly negative, corners are strongly negative. The prey now has an unambiguous incentive to stay in open space and escape corners immediately when it drifts into one.
