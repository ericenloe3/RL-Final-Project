Final-Run Comparison: PPO vs. SAC
Headline outcome
After 10M steps, the final policies sit on opposite sides of the performance landscape. PPO converges to a 100% capture rate with hunter return +109 and prey return −89. SAC settles at 14.4% capture rate with hunter return +3.4 and prey return +15. The evaluation panel confirms this generalises: PPO captures in 13/15 evaluation episodes (87%), SAC in 2/15 (13%). The episode-length distribution is the cleanest summary — PPO's 13 captures cluster between 50 and 320 steps with median ~215, while SAC's two captures sit at 140 and 260 and the other 13 episodes are all timeouts at exactly 500.
What the evaluation chart also reveals — and what's worth highlighting to a peer audience — is that SAC's prey learned something the PPO prey never did. The LOS-breaks panel shows SAC's mean of 11.9 breaks per episode is dominated by a single episode with 154 breaks, and PPO's mean of 38.1 by two episodes (179, 243) where the hunter circled while the prey orbited an obstacle. Both methods produce occasional high-LOS-break runs but neither consistently. That said, the SAC prey is genuinely competitive in the few episodes where it survives — the hunter closing distance histogram shows SAC's hunter advanced only 154 px on average compared to PPO's 334 px. The SAC prey isn't bad at evasion; it's living in front of a hunter that never developed real pursuit competence.
The training dynamics tell two completely different stories
The capture-rate panel is striking when you read it as a function of training events rather than just time. SAC reaches 100% capture at 1.45M steps — well before PPO has even unfrozen its hunter. Then SAC begins falling, crossing 50% by ~3M and stabilising near 15% from 5M onward. PPO crosses SAC at ~2.5M (right after the hunter unfreeze) and climbs steadily to 100% by 3.5M, where it stays for the next 6M steps.
The hunter-return curves directly mirror this: SAC hunter peaks at +119 and decays to +3.4. PPO hunter starts negative during the freeze, ramps to +109 after unfreezing, and holds. The prey curves are the inverse — SAC prey recovers from −65 back to +15, PPO prey settles at a stable −89.
This is not "two algorithms learning at different rates." It's two algorithms ending up in qualitatively different equilibria.

Problem 1: PPO's prey entropy keeps growing without bound
The PPO entropy panel shows what was diagnosed in an earlier session and then partially fixed: hunter entropy stays in a healthy range (2–3 nats, decaying to 0.5 by end of training as the policy sharpens). Prey entropy grows linearly from 3 nats at start to 54 nats by 10M steps — and the actual numbers from the log show this is monotonic, never plateauing.
54 nats for a 2D Gaussian implies log_std ≈ 26, i.e. action standard deviation around exp(26) ≈ 10¹¹. The sampled actions are essentially random Gaussian noise on an astronomical scale, then clipped to [-1, 1] at the action boundary. The prey has been sampling near-uniform random actions for most of training.
The reason this didn't tank PPO's overall performance is that the deterministic mean policy is what gets used at evaluation time, and the mean network can still learn from advantage signals as long as the gradient isn't completely drowned in noise. But the implications are real:

The prey return is genuinely −89 (capture penalty). The prey isn't successfully evading because it's not actually executing its mean policy during training rollouts — it's flailing.
The "training" the prey is getting after step ~2M is mostly entropy-bonus-driven random exploration that never converts into improved mean actions, because the gradient signal is dominated by the entropy term.
The fact that PPO still wins the comparison says more about the simplicity of the hunter's task than about good prey training.

The fix from the earlier session (clamp log_std to [-3, 0.5] and reduce prey ENT_COEF end from 0.015 to 0.005) targets exactly this — and the log values you have here are from a run that didn't apply that fix yet.

Problem 2: SAC hunter α collapses early and never recovers
The SAC alpha panel shows both temperatures dropping from 1.0 to near-zero very quickly. The numbers from the log are precise: hunter α drops below 0.05 at step 670,000 — well within the freeze period. Prey α drops faster, hitting 0.005 by step 50,000.
α near zero means the entropy bonus in the actor loss is gone. The actor then optimises purely for min(Q1, Q2), which produces a deterministic policy. This is exactly the deterministic-orbit failure that motivated the v5 fixes (raising target_entropy to −1, clearing the buffer on unfreeze). The fact that α still collapses despite those fixes tells you something important: the target_entropy of −1 is still too low for this problem.
The mechanism is that α is updated by α_loss = −log_α × (log_prob + target_entropy). When the policy's actual entropy exceeds the target, the gradient pushes α down. With target = −1 and a 2D action space, the policy reaches that target very quickly because tanh-squashed Gaussians with reasonable std easily exceed −1 nats of entropy per dimension. The temperature collapses, the policy becomes deterministic, and SAC loses its exploration mechanism right when it needs it most — at the moment co-evolution starts.
You can see the consequences directly in the capture rate. SAC peaks at 100% at step 1.45M when the hunter is good enough to catch a prey that hasn't yet learned cover-seeking. Then the prey starts learning, the hunter has no exploration budget left to adapt, and the capture rate falls off a cliff. By 5M steps it's stabilised around 15% and the system is stuck in a bad local equilibrium where the prey runs predictable patterns the hunter could counter if it weren't locked into its own deterministic policy.

The deeper pattern
Both failures share a structure: the entropy-control mechanism in each algorithm has a degenerate solution that the optimiser finds. PPO's prey has unbounded log_std and the entropy bonus is large, so the gradient pushes log_std to infinity rather than improving the mean. SAC's α has a target that's too low, so the entropy bonus disappears entirely and the policy collapses.
In PPO this is a local failure — the prey degenerates while the hunter wins anyway. In SAC it's a system-level failure because both agents share the same off-policy buffer ecosystem: once the hunter collapses, the prey's training distribution collapses with it, and the system never recovers. This is consistent with the broader observation that off-policy methods are more sensitive to early-training dynamics than on-policy methods, because every bad sample lives in the buffer for ~62k subsequent gradient steps (1M buffer / 16 envs) and the critic uses them all.
The two practical recommendations are clear:

Reapply the PPO log_std clamp. This is the v6 fix we already designed but hasn't been applied to the run shown here. Without it, the prey is permanently in noise mode after step ~2M.
Raise SAC target_entropy further, to around 0 (or higher). At target = 0 each dimension is allowed roughly 0.5 nats, which gives a tanh-Gaussian std around 0.6 — enough for genuine continuing exploration while still letting the policy be reasonably committed to its mean. Combined with monitoring α and rolling back to a checkpoint if it collapses below ~0.02, this would give SAC a fair shot at sustained learning.
