Two specific failure modes identified
Failure 1: Parameter sharing produces correlated policies. Both hunters use the same network. Given similar observations (each sees the prey + their teammate), they produce similar action gradients and end up on the same side of the prey. The policy never learned to differentiate based on teammate position because nothing in the reward structure explicitly says "opposite sides is better than same side."
Failure 2: The search-reward cutoff creates a flat-valley landscape. The hunter's "search toward last-known prey position" reward fires only when staleness < 100. After that, when the prey has been hidden for 100+ steps, the hunter has no per-step gradient pushing it anywhere. The policy converges to "do nothing" because doing something costs the −0.01 step penalty without any prospective reward. That's why the hunters freeze.
Three fixes implemented
Fix 1: R_FLANK = 0.02 per step per hunter (env.py). Computes the dot product of unit vectors prey → h1 and prey → h2. When that's negative (hunters on opposite sides), each hunter gets R_FLANK × |dot| reward. Maximum +0.02 when perfectly flanking, zero when same-side or orthogonal. Forces the shared policy to learn "if my teammate is on the prey's left, I should approach from the right" — the explicit coordination signal that 2v1 needs.
Fix 2: Smooth the search-reward cutoff (env.py). Old design: search reward fires at full strength for staleness < 100, then drops to zero. New design: linear decay from full strength at stale=0 to zero at stale=300. Always some gradient toward the last-known prey position, never an abrupt cliff. Verified in the smoke test — the reward at staleness=290 is now +0.106 instead of zero, keeping the hunter active rather than frozen.
Fix 3: SAC buffer size 1M → 500k (train_sac.py). The peak-then-collapse pattern is driven by the off-policy critic fitting an outdated prey distribution while the prey is actively learning. Halving the buffer halves the average staleness of training samples, helping the critic track the current prey policy faster. Trade-off: slightly less sample efficiency, but stability is what we need at this scale.
What you should see in the next run
The diagnostic to watch is trajectory flank score over time within an episode. In v2, the typical trajectory was: start flanking → cluster on one side → freeze. In v3, with the R_FLANK reward continuously rewarding spread, the trajectory should: start flanking → maintain flanking → close in alternately.
The hunter-frozen pattern in eval timeouts should disappear: even when the prey is hidden for hundreds of steps, the search-reward decay means hunters keep gradually moving toward where they last saw it, not standing still.
The capture rate trajectory should be more stable post-peak. v2 went 100% → 19% over 7M steps; v3's smaller buffer should make the critic adapt faster to the prey's evolution, ideally landing the final capture rate somewhere in the 40-70% range rather than crashing to ~20%.
If the LOS-cycling exploit (200+ breaks per episode in some v2 evals) reappears in v3 evals, that's a sign the prey has learned to game the new R_HIDDEN per-step bonus instead of R_LOS_BREAK. The next move would be to anneal the prey's cover-related rewards to zero in late training.
