The numbers confirm the diagnosis. The prey runs in a straight line (0.83–0.99 directional consistency), ends up at walls (35–55% of frames), and never deliberately uses obstacles for cover. Here are the concrete next steps, ranked by impact:
1. LOS-break reward — teach the prey that hiding works
This is the single most impactful addition. Right now the prey gets the same heading reward whether it runs into open space or ducks behind an obstacle. A one-time bonus when LOS transitions from clear to blocked would teach the prey that breaking line-of-sight is valuable. Crucially this should be a transition reward (fires once when LOS changes from 1→0), not a sustained reward for staying hidden — otherwise the prey learns to freeze behind cover instead of actively evading.
Something like +0.5 each time LOS transitions 1→0 (about 5× the per-step heading reward, so it's a strong one-time incentive to seek cover, but doesn't overwhelm the heading signal over time).
2. Direction-to-nearest-cover observation
The prey has obstacle features (positions, sizes, distances), but it doesn't have a pre-computed "which direction is the nearest obstacle relative to the hunter's approach direction." Adding a 2D unit vector pointing from the prey toward the nearest obstacle that's roughly between the prey and the hunter would give the policy a much cleaner signal for "where to dodge." Right now the prey has to figure out the spatial relationship between 5 obstacles, itself, and the hunter from raw coordinates — that's a hard computation for a small network to learn.
3. Reward for heading toward cover when the hunter is close
A small bonus when the prey moves toward a nearby obstacle (dot product of prey displacement with direction-to-nearest-obstacle > 0) while the hunter is within a threat radius. This bridges the gap between "flee in a straight line" and "flee toward cover." It should be small (~0.03/step, much less than the heading reward) so it doesn't override the primary escape signal but tilts the tiebreaker toward cover-seeking.
4. Corner-avoidance through observation, not penalty
The prey gets trapped at walls because it can't see them coming until it hits them. The wall-distance observations we just added help, but you could also add a "remaining escape angle" feature — what fraction of the semicircle away from the hunter is open vs blocked by walls. Near a corner, this fraction drops to ~25%, which the policy can learn means "bad place to be."
5. Longer training and more diverse spawns
5M steps is enough for basic pursuit-evasion but tactical cover use requires the prey to discover through random exploration that LOS-breaking leads to longer survival. This is a rare event in early training — the prey has to randomly move behind an obstacle, then notice the hunter took a detour. Increasing to 8–10M steps, or using a higher initial entropy coefficient (0.03 instead of 0.01), gives more exploration time for this discovery.
6. Prey-specific curriculum
In the current curriculum, both agents experience the same obstacle schedule. You could add a prey-favoring phase where obstacles are placed between the agents at reset, so the prey spawns near cover. This gives the prey early experience with LOS-breaking situations, accelerating its learning.
