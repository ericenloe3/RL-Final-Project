What happenedThe numbers tell the story:
Capture rate: 100% by step ~3.5M and stays there. The hunters dominate completely.
Prey return locked at −90 for the entire second half of training (steps 3M → 10M). That's the capture penalty floor — the prey was caught nearly every episode and gained no learning signal during those episodes.
Capture splits evenly: h1 ≈ 50%, h2 ≈ 50%. Parameter sharing worked — both hunters contribute equally.
Average episode length: 119–135 steps in the late training phase. The prey survives ~130 of 600 steps before being caught, then the episode resets. That's 22% of the available episode length.
Prey log_std pinned at +0.5 (the upper clamp) from step 200k onward — for 9.8M of the 10M total steps. The clamp is doing exactly what it was designed to do (preventing the unbounded growth that broke v6), but it's also flagging that the prey training has degenerated.
Looking at the GIFs corroborates this: the prey isn't using the obstacles as cover at all. In episode 4, the prey is stuck against the top wall with one hunter directly below it and the other approaching — no attempt to dodge around the obstacles to its right. In episode 5, the prey is on the bottom-right wedged against an obstacle while both hunters approach in formation. It's running mostly in straight lines and getting cornered.Why the prey lost the abilities it had in 1v1Three reasons combine:1. The advantage signal collapsed. In 1v1, when the prey occasionally evaded the hunter, those rare success episodes produced large positive advantages that the policy gradient could lock onto. In 2v1 with 100% capture rate, every episode is a loss — all prey advantages are negative. PPO's normalised advantages then have nearly zero variance and minimal directional information; the policy gradient is dominated by the entropy bonus.2. The clamp at +0.5 became the equilibrium. Once log_std hits the upper bound and the policy gradient is too weak to push it down, the prey's actions are sampled from a Gaussian with std ≈ 1.65 — large noise relative to the action range. With purely negative advantages, sharpening the mean policy hurts (any specific action gets caught), so the gradient never overcomes the entropy push at the boundary. The prey is effectively stuck behaving randomly.3. The 2v1 problem is fundamentally harder than 1v1 with only a 4.5/4.5 speed setup. Two hunters at equal speed can geometrically pinch the prey from converging angles regardless of the prey's strategy — there's no "perfect" escape direction. The cover-seeking behaviour that worked in 1v1 only delays capture by a few seconds rather than enabling escape.Should the episode length be shortened?You asked specifically about this. Yes, but for the prey-training reason rather than the fairness reason you suggested. Let me explain.Shortening episodes from 600 → 400 steps does not make it "more fair for the prey." Whether captured at step 130 or step 130 of a shorter episode, the prey gets the same negative reward. Episode length doesn't change the per-step game dynamics.What it does change is the rate of episode completions. At avg_ep_steps ≈ 130, with NUM_ENVS × NUM_STEPS = 4096 env steps per update, you're getting ~31 episodes per update. Each PPO update therefore gets only ~31 terminal-reward signals to learn from. Shorter max_steps makes timeouts happen sooner, which would only matter if the prey were currently surviving to timeout — but it isn't. Captures are limiting episode length, not max_steps.The right diagnosis here is that the prey needs survival episodes to exist at all so it has positive trajectories to learn from. Lowering max_steps doesn't help with that. What helps is rebalancing the difficulty so the prey occasionally escapes — through the changes I'll make below.Fixes to implementFive targeted changes:
Curriculum: re-introduce the speed ramp. Start hunters at 4.0 (slower than prey's 4.5) and ramp to 4.5 over training. Gives the prey a winning environment for early phases so it can develop policies before facing 2v1 at full speed.
Add a small per-hunter speed advantage at the end (4.4 vs 4.5). A tiny prey-favouring asymmetry that keeps "escape is possible" as the reward landscape, even when the hunters are coordinated.
Lower prey end entropy coefficient further (0.005 → 0.001). The current value is high enough to dominate the prey's gradient when advantages are weak.
Tighten prey log_std upper clamp from +0.5 to +0.0 (std 1.65 → 1.0). Less noise headroom, forcing the policy to commit to its mean.
Shorten max_steps 600 → 450. Justification: the average successful episode in 1v1 v7 was ~225 steps; 450 is double that, generous but no longer wastefully long. This isn't about fairness — it's about getting more episode resets (and therefore more terminal-reward signals) per update, which accelerates prey learning.
